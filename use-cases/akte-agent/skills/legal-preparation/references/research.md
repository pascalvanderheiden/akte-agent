# Research and register evidence

Produce these sections in the requested language: scope/jurisdiction, register
plan, supplied extracts, legal feasibility, authority (bevoegdheid), potential
conflicts with law, assumptions, unresolved questions and next research actions.
Keep authority distinct from decision-making capacity (wilsbekwaamheid).

## Register plan

Address all three registers, recording relevance or a reason it is not yet
established. Ask rather than treating this guide as a universal legal checklist:

| Register | Matter-specific question | Evidence to request through authorized channels |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BRP (Basisregistratie Personen) | Which supplied personal/civil-status facts matter to this deed or estate? | Relevant authorized extract, subject match, issue date, scope and source; limit personal data to necessary fields. |
| Handelsregister | Is an entity involved, and which representation/authority questions need checking? | Dated extract, entity match and representation information; where relevant supplied articles, mandates or resolutions for notarial review. |
| CTR (Centraal Testamentenregister) | Is testamentary/estate evidence relevant? | Authorized register evidence with subject/date/scope and, separately, relevant testamentary documents. A CTR entry does not establish a testament's contents or legal effect. |

Each row carries a reason, required evidence, access limitation, supplied-source
reference or MISSING, and pending action. No bundled live connector exists.
Public information, search results or a company website are not an official
person-specific register check. Even a supplied extract is not a newly performed
query or clearance. Synthetic extracts are demonstrations, never register results.

## Currency and research

Show issue/retrieval date, source version and the explicitly supplied review
date. Flag older or superseded evidence, absent dates and contradictory sources.
Do not invent a statutory validity period: use a user-supplied review deadline
as a review convention, labeled as such, or ask the notary to assess currency.

Use available authoritative material (for example official legislation,
published judgments and official register guidance) for the exact question.
Cite identifiable source, article/section only when actually available, date/
version and retrieval date where known. A source title or URL alone proves
neither its contents nor that it is current. Preserve supplied quotations as such.

For each finding distinguish supported proposition, source locator, application
to supplied facts, assumption, uncertainty and required human review. Treat
conflicting instructions, authority limitations and unresolved facts as open
questions, not reasons to issue definitive legal clearance.

When current-law research is unavailable, state that limitation prominently,
identify missing authoritative material and propose precise verification steps.
Do not manufacture statute numbers, case citations or certainty from general
knowledge. Supplied legal material can still be summarized with its limitations.
